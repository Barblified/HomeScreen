4 · Four-point-hit break-even thresholds

Since the Saka → Rogers move used the GW8 free transfer, another move deducts four points at the GW8 deadline under official FPL transfer rules. A correct single-week team comparison is:

Net E[ΔGW8] = E[best legal XI after transfer] − E[best legal HOLD XI] − 4 − change in expected captain/auto-sub effects (if not already incorporated in the XI difference).

Where roles and lineups are held fixed for a simple player replacement, this simplifies to xPts(in) − xPts(out) − 4. For Fernandes: 4.9 + 4 = 8.9 xPts is the immediate one-for-one break-even requirement. Since none of the examined midfielders clears it, none is justified by GW8 base points alone. For a Wissa → Thiago substitution that upgrades the final XI from a 3.9-point player to 5.0, the illustrative single-week net is 5.0 − 3.9 − 4 = −2.9 points.

Important distinction: Wissa is a potential bench player. His replacement cannot earn ordinary XI points unless selected ahead of someone else. If the best HOLD uses a different formation, one must compare the two optimised XIs, not just forward-versus-forward rates. This example is conditional and not a defensible joint simulation.

5 · HOLD today versus the exact same replacement in GW9

Comparing permanent ownership to another permanent ownership path artificially favours transferring early because it omits the ability to change players later. A better paired path holds all else fixed:

  • Path A (NOW): Buy replacement before GW8, incur −4, keep him after GW8.
  • Path B (WAIT): Field the current player in GW8, then use next week's one free transfer on the same replacement for GW9 onward.

With unchanged players, no future injuries, no price constraints and identical choices GW9 onward, the difference across all 1/3/5-week horizons is driven by just GW8:

E[V(NOW) − V(WAIT)] = E[Pin,8 − Pout,8] − 4.

For a Fernandes → Ødegaard example this is 4.8 − 4.9 − 4 = −4.1 points. For Fernandes → Groß, −5.1 points. For a lineup-enabled Wissa → Thiago scenario, −2.9 points. The raw five-week player comparison is not the decision's five-week incremental value when a free GW9 substitution exists.

What can flip this? A price change making GW9's intended transaction impossible, another urgent GW9 injury that consumes the FT, material changes to starter minutes, captaincy or a replacement with more than a four-point additional GW8 edge. Those are genuine state/option effects and would need explicit probabilities/conditional plan valuation—not fabricated values.

7 · Decision theory: theorems actually relevant

Bellman's principle of optimality

For squad state s, action a, current points P, paid hits H and updated future state S', the sequential objective is V(s) = max_a E[P(s,a) − 4H(a) + V(S')]. This shows why a five-week optimisation must allow GW9 actions. Raw “buy X for five weeks vs keep Y for five weeks” is insufficient.

Value of information

When an action may be delayed without other cost, E[max_a U(a,I)] ≥ max_a E[U(a,I)]: after receiving injury or manager information I, the manager can choose the better action. This is a consequence of convexity/Jensen's inequality. It does not prove waiting wins once price rises, injuries, and foregone GW8 points are included.

Small-sample shrinkage

A sensible position/team prior for the 2026/27 season shrinks noisy seven-week per-90 rates toward a current-season baseline: r_post = (m·r_obs + κ·r_prior)/(m + κ), where m is minutes and κ is prior-equivalent minutes. This guards against overreacting to Groß's short-run 58-point start or Wissa's low realised returns. The equation is a method specification; no new posterior rate is claimed.
