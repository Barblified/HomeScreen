3. Model actually used

The forecast is fitted only to GW1–7 of 2026/27. For each player it estimates minutes/availability state, expected-goal and expected-assist rates, clean-sheet probability, save rate, cards, bonus and 2026/27 defensive-contribution points. Player xG/xA rates are shrunk toward current-season positional medians with a weak 360-minute pseudo-sample, then adjusted by fixture difficulty and mapped through official FPL scoring.

Why shrinkage matters: the posterior rate is a weighted average of observed and positional rates, r̂ = (M·r + κ·rpos)/(M+κ). This is the James–Stein/Bayesian shrinkage intuition: with only seven matches, extreme scorers such as Groß or De Cuyper are likely to include outcome noise. It prevents buying last week's points as if they were a stable future rate.

After defining those means, 20,000 common-random-number simulations jointly draw availability, shared team shocks and over-dispersed scoring, then apply legal formations, captain/vice fallback and ordered autosubs. Ordinary bench points are excluded unless an autosub is triggered. The same random draws compare HOLD and transfer paths, reducing Monte Carlo noise.

Uncertainty is separated: aleatoric uncertainty is the match/scoring randomness inside the simulation; state uncertainty is availability, minutes and captain/autosub state; epistemic uncertainty is the seven-match parameter/model uncertainty, addressed by shrinkage, walk-forward testing and a realised-points sensitivity model. The displayed 80% intervals include simulated aleatoric/state variation; they do not pretend all epistemic uncertainty has disappeared.
