NEW · 4 NOVEMBER

Energy Independence Bill introduced with consumer and resilience reforms

The government introduced the Energy Independence Bill at first reading. Its published package would strengthen Ofgem enforcement, regulate energy brokers, place part of the Renewables Obligation transfer to public spending on a longer footing, extend smart-meter powers, reform grid and land-access rules, end new oil-and-gas exploration licences and add powers covering gas and LPG supply resilience.

Why it matters: The Bill joins household affordability, consumer redress, infrastructure delivery and energy security in one legislative vehicle. Those are long-term policy pathways, not an immediate bill cut or supply guarantee.

Evidence / uncertainty: The bill record, government overview and detailed factsheets confirm introduction and scope. Many effects depend on amendments, secondary legislation, regulatory decisions and delivery; stated savings and benefits are government estimates, not observed outcomes.

NEW · 4 NOVEMBER

Government formally intervenes in proposed Sky–ITV deal

The Culture Secretary has issued a Public Interest Intervention Notice over Sky’s proposed £1.6 billion acquisition of ITV’s media and entertainment business. The CMA will examine jurisdiction and competition, while Ofcom will assess news plurality, free expression and control of media enterprises; both are due to report by 27 January 2027. The minister also proposes extending the statutory public-interest test from broadcasting to on-demand programming.

Why it matters: This is a formal regulatory step, not a prohibition. The outcome could affect competition, the range of UK news voices and the structure of public-service broadcasting and streaming.

Evidence / uncertainty: The ministerial statement is corroborated by Reuters and AP. No final competition or public-interest finding has been made, and the deal remains subject to the regulators’ reports.

NEW · 4 NOVEMBER

Government sets out statutory safeguards for police facial recognition

The Home Office says the Police Reform Bill will create a statutory framework and code of practice for police biometric and overt-surveillance systems, plus a new independent oversight body with monitoring and enforcement powers. The consultation drew more than 13,800 responses and also prompted proposed complementary rules for some private-sector crime-prevention uses.

Why it matters: Clearer law and oversight could reduce the current patchwork of rules while allowing wider use of a powerful identification tool. The balance between public protection, proportionality, privacy and discrimination remains material.

Evidence / uncertainty: The official announcement and ministerial statement establish the policy direction. Independent specialist reporting notes that specific limits on access, retention, protest use and scope creep remain under-defined; the legislation and code have not yet completed scrutiny.
