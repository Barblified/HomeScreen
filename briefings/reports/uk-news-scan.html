UK Economy & Business

Business-cost package meets a weaker September for retail volumes

New · 23 October The government estimates that simpler reporting and compliance, extended tariff suspensions and property-transaction changes will save firms almost £800 million a year. Separately, the first estimate for September retail sales showed volumes down 0.4% month on month, weaker than the 0.1% fall in the Reuters poll. [42][43][44][45]

Why it matters: Lower administrative and input costs may help margins, but the retail result is an observed sign of softer demand ahead of the Budget.

Evidence / uncertainty: The retail estimate is accredited official statistics; the savings are policy estimates, not cash grants or audited realised savings. Delivery details and business uptake will determine the effect.

Household risk pathway: Indirect through prices, jobs and local business activity; no emergency action is indicated.

Sources: [42] [43] [44] [45]

Flash PMI signals stronger activity and renewed hiring

New release · 23 October, 09:30 S&P Global’s flash composite output index rose to 53.1 in October from 52.0 in September, its strongest reading since February and above every forecast in Reuters’ poll. Firms reported growing orders, the first net hiring increase since the 2024 tax rise and the fastest manufacturing-output growth in more than two years. [49][50]

Why it matters: The survey is counterevidence to an immediate downturn, but it also recorded the strongest cost inflation since June, reinforcing expectations of higher interest rates.

Evidence / uncertainty: The primary flash release and Reuters agree. PMI measures survey responses rather than realised GDP, and the final reading can be revised.

Household risk pathway: Potentially supportive for jobs, but higher input costs and rate expectations can still reach prices and borrowing.

Sources: [49] [50]

Consumer confidence records its sharpest monthly fall since the pandemic

New · 23 October GfK’s October index fell 11 points to −24 from −13, below forecasts. The survey covered 2,003 adults from 1–14 October; the major-purchase measure reached its lowest in more than two years and inflation expectations rose. [11][12]

Why it matters: Sentiment can weaken discretionary spending and adds pressure before the Budget. It is not realised sales or a recession measure.

Evidence / uncertainty: Independent reports agree on the fall. Retail, labour and GDP data will show whether sentiment translates into activity.

Household risk pathway: Moderate through spending restraint and borrowing expectations; no emergency action.

Sources: [11] [12]

Inflation and borrowing narrow the room for manoeuvre before the Budget

Retained · 21 October CPI rose to 3.5% in September, with motor fuels the largest upward contribution. Public-sector net borrowing excluding public-sector banks reached £24.52bn; debt passed £3tn in cash terms but was around 94% of GDP, lower than a year earlier. [17][18][19][20]

Why it matters: Fuel costs already reach households and businesses, while borrowing constrains fiscal choices. Cash debt alone does not measure affordability.

Evidence / uncertainty: ONS data are observed; persistence depends on oil, sterling, services inflation, growth and Budget choices.

Household risk pathway: Moderate through fuel, freight and possible interest-rate expectations.

Sources: [17] [18] [19] [20]

Winter outlooks project sufficient gas and electricity capacity

New reports · 23 October National Gas says peak supply capability should exceed forecast peak gas demand by 99 million cubic metres a day. NESO expects 5.9GW of electricity capacity above peak demand, about 9.5%. Both assessments allow for stress testing; NESO says tight days may prompt routine market notices, not a forecast of blackouts. [46][47][48]

Why it matters: The reports separate physical supply resilience from the distinct risk of elevated prices driven by global gas and oil markets.

Evidence / uncertainty: These are primary operator forecasts corroborated by Reuters, not guarantees against local faults, extreme combinations or price volatility.

Household risk pathway: No case for stockpiling; ordinary outage planning and budget awareness remain proportionate.

Sources: [46] [47] [48]

Government proposes stronger law for critical undersea infrastructure

Retained · 22 October A consultation proposes modernised offences, up to 14 years’ imprisonment for deliberate damage, duties for subsea telecoms providers and national-security direction powers. The evidence base says undersea cables carry about 99% of UK international data. [27]

Why it matters: Cable and pipeline disruption can cascade into communications, finance and energy; most damage remains accidental.

Evidence / uncertainty: These are proposals, not enacted law.

Household risk pathway: Structural rather than immediate; retain ordinary offline contacts and payment fallback.
