Government launches three-year Be Ready household-resilience campaign

New · 22–23 October The campaign asks households to plan for up to three days of disruption, keep a modest rotating supply of water and food, record key contacts and consider the Priority Services Register. Ministers describe the advice as low-cost preparation for everyday emergencies such as severe weather, not a forecast of a specific crisis. [67][68]

Why it matters: It moves household resilience from evergreen guidance into a sustained public-information programme.

Evidence / uncertainty: The launch and practical advice are official and independently reported; campaign reach and behaviour change are not yet measurable.

Household risk pathway: Build a buffer gradually around normal shopping and individual needs; no panic buying or exceptional spending is indicated.

UK Economy & Business

Business-cost package meets a weaker September for retail volumes

New · 23 October The government estimates that simpler reporting and compliance, extended tariff suspensions and property-transaction changes will save firms almost £800 million a year. Separately, the first estimate for September retail sales showed volumes down 0.4% month on month, weaker than the 0.1% fall in the Reuters poll. [38][39][40][41]

Why it matters: Lower administrative and input costs may help margins, but the retail result is an observed sign of softer demand ahead of the Budget.

Evidence / uncertainty: The retail estimate is accredited official statistics; the savings are policy estimates, not cash grants or audited realised savings. Delivery details and business uptake will determine the effect.

Household risk pathway: Indirect through prices, jobs and local business activity; no emergency action is indicated.

Sources: [38] [39] [40] [41]

Flash PMI signals stronger activity and renewed hiring

Updated market reaction · 23 October S&P Global’s flash composite output index rose to 53.1 in October from 52.0 in September, its strongest reading since February and above every forecast in Reuters’ poll. Firms reported growing orders, the first net hiring increase since the 2024 tax rise and the fastest manufacturing-output growth in more than two years. Sterling subsequently touched a 17-month high against the euro, although it eased against a strengthening dollar. [45][46][52]

Why it matters: The survey and market response are counterevidence to an immediate downturn, but the PMI also recorded the strongest cost inflation since June, reinforcing expectations of higher interest rates.

Evidence / uncertainty: The primary flash release and Reuters agree. PMI measures survey responses rather than realised GDP, and exchange rates are volatile.

Household risk pathway: Potentially supportive for jobs and some import costs, but higher input costs and rate expectations can still reach prices and borrowing.

Sources: [45] [46] [52]

Consumer confidence records its sharpest monthly fall since the pandemic

New · 23 October GfK’s October index fell 11 points to −24 from −13, below forecasts. The survey covered 2,003 adults from 1–14 October; the major-purchase measure reached its lowest in more than two years and inflation expectations rose. [11][12]

Why it matters: Sentiment can weaken discretionary spending and adds pressure before the Budget. It is not realised sales or a recession measure.

Evidence / uncertainty: Independent reports agree on the fall. Retail, labour and GDP data will show whether sentiment translates into activity.

Household risk pathway: Moderate through spending restraint and borrowing expectations; no emergency action.
