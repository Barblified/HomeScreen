UK Economy & Business

Business-cost package meets a weaker September for retail volumes

New · 23 October The government estimates that simpler reporting and compliance, extended tariff suspensions and property-transaction changes will save firms almost £800 million a year. Separately, the first estimate for September retail sales showed volumes down 0.4% month on month, weaker than the 0.1% fall in the Reuters poll. [42][43][44][45]

Why it matters: Lower administrative and input costs may help margins, but the retail result is an observed sign of softer demand ahead of the Budget.

Evidence / uncertainty: The retail estimate is accredited official statistics; the savings are policy estimates, not cash grants or audited realised savings. Delivery details and business uptake will determine the effect.

Household risk pathway: Indirect through prices, jobs and local business activity; no emergency action is indicated.

Flash PMI signals stronger activity and renewed hiring

Updated market reaction · 23 October S&P Global’s flash composite output index rose to 53.1 in October from 52.0 in September, its strongest reading since February and above every forecast in Reuters’ poll. Firms reported growing orders, the first net hiring increase since the 2024 tax rise and the fastest manufacturing-output growth in more than two years. Sterling subsequently touched a 17-month high against the euro, although it eased against a strengthening dollar. [49][50][56]

Why it matters: The survey and market response are counterevidence to an immediate downturn, but the PMI also recorded the strongest cost inflation since June, reinforcing expectations of higher interest rates.

Evidence / uncertainty: The primary flash release and Reuters agree. PMI measures survey responses rather than realised GDP, and exchange rates are volatile.

Household risk pathway: Potentially supportive for jobs and some import costs, but higher input costs and rate expectations can still reach prices and borrowing.

Sources: [49] [50] [56]

Evidence / uncertainty: Independent reports agree on the fall. Retail, labour and GDP data will show whether sentiment translates into activity.

Inflation and borrowing narrow the room for manoeuvre before the Budget

Retained · 21 October CPI rose to 3.5% in September, with motor fuels the largest upward contribution. Public-sector net borrowing excluding public-sector banks reached £24.52bn; debt passed £3tn in cash terms but was around 94% of GDP, lower than a year earlier. [17][18][19][20]

Evidence / uncertainty: ONS data are observed; persistence depends on oil, sterling, services inflation, growth and Budget choices.

Household risk pathway: Moderate through fuel, freight and possible interest-rate expectations.
