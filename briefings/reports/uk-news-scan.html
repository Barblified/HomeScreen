UK Politics & Public Affairs

NEW · 4 NOVEMBER

Government formally intervenes in proposed Sky–ITV deal

The Culture Secretary has issued a Public Interest Intervention Notice over Sky’s proposed £1.6 billion acquisition of ITV’s media and entertainment business. The CMA will examine jurisdiction and competition, while Ofcom will assess news plurality, free expression and control of media enterprises; both are due to report by 27 January 2027. The minister also proposes extending the statutory public-interest test from broadcasting to on-demand programming.

Why it matters: This is a formal regulatory step, not a prohibition. The outcome could affect competition, the range of UK news voices and the structure of public-service broadcasting and streaming.

Evidence / uncertainty: The ministerial statement is corroborated by Reuters and AP. No final competition or public-interest finding has been made, and the deal remains subject to the regulators’ reports.

Commons approves Budget resolutions; detailed scrutiny continues

The Commons voting record shows majorities for resolutions including a temporary bank surcharge increase, the VAT treatment of public electric-vehicle charging and a high-value council-tax surcharge. The Budget document says the bank surcharge rises from 3% to 6% from January 2027 to March 2028 and clarifies 20% VAT on public charging from 1 November. Why it matters: The measures affect fiscal distribution, public-charging costs and some high-value properties, but do not constitute an immediate preparedness hazard.

Evidence / uncertainty: Primary parliamentary and Treasury material is corroborated by an independent division record. Implementation details may change through legislation and administration.

Government factsheets updated on 3 November say the regime would bring qualifying data centres, managed service providers, large load controllers and designated critical suppliers into scope, with an initial incident notification within 24 hours and a fuller report within 72 hours.

Evidence / uncertainty: The parliamentary stage and policy design are verified primary facts. The bill is not yet proof of improved resilience and does not signal a current cyber incident.

NCSC says AI is accelerating cyber intrusion and CNI defenders should prepare
