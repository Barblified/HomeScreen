Green Book reform will let linked local projects be judged together

New 15:41 BST The government says revised public-investment rules will allow local and regional authorities to bundle linked projects such as housing and transport into one place-based business case. The changes, due to be set out in the 28 October Budget, build on a reduction in the Green Book’s headline discount rate from 3.5% to 3%. The Budget is also expected to include a fiscal-devolution roadmap. [102][103]

Why it matters: Appraising connected projects together could improve the viability of regional programmes whose benefits depend on one another, including outside London. It changes the appraisal framework, not the availability of money by itself.

Evidence / uncertainty: The official announcement and independent Reuters report agree on the core mechanism. The detailed Budget text, departmental guidance, funding envelopes and audit of real project decisions will determine whether the reform materially changes regional investment.

Threat-to-household assessment: Potentially important for regional growth and infrastructure over years, but no immediate household action follows.

Covid Inquiry says adult social care entered the pandemic structurally exposed

New The Inquiry’s Module 6 report, published today, covers publicly and privately funded adult social care in all four UK nations. It examines government decisions, infection control, hospital discharge, workforce capacity and unpaid care. Its factual record supports a broad point of agreement: social care lacked the status, information flows, infection-control capacity and protective equipment available to the NHS. Evidence / uncertainty: The official report is the primary finding; criticism is attributed, not treated as a competing factual account. The inquiry establishes institutional lessons, not criminal liability. Evidence that would change the assessment includes funded implementation timetables, enforceable visiting safeguards and audited stock/workforce benchmarks.

Threat-to-household assessment: Medium-term and structural, particularly for people dependent on care. No new outbreak or immediate household action follows from publication.

The regulator is consulting separately on licence changes and a cost-recovery mechanism. It says the links are used only occasionally for imports but provide flexibility during unusually high demand; recent import capability had fallen because of weaker commercial incentives and technical reductions. [79][80]

Why it matters: Preserving an existing contingency route improves system optionality over coming winters. It neither creates a new gas field nor demonstrates a current shortage; any household cost effect depends on the still-open funding consultation and future market conditions.

Evidence / uncertainty: The approval and consultation are primary Ofgem facts, independently reported by Reuters. Ofgem and National Gas say normal-condition supply remains secure. Stress-scenario value, final cost recovery and actual future utilisation remain uncertain.

Critical PHP flaws: 32 government services offline during mitigation

Materially updated 16:46 BST · current critical advisory The NCSC warns that CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 can permit unauthenticated remote code execution and may affect all PHP versions. PHP released fixed versions on 21 October and says no complete mitigation substitutes for updating. Cloudflare told Reuters that exploit attempts rose into the millions. Technology minister Liz Lloyd told the House of Lords that 32 government services were unavailable and four partly unavailable, largely because systems were taken down precautionarily while security updates and mitigations were applied. A UK cyber-security official later said there were no indications of successful exploitation. The IPO separately confirms a related deposit-account service issue. [91][92][93][94][95][96]

Why it matters: PHP underpins many websites and hosted services, so successful compromise can propagate into data exposure, service interruption and downstream supplier risk. The affected authority is each service operator, including organisations whose products bundle PHP.

Evidence / uncertainty: The vulnerability, fixed releases, exploitation attempts and precautionary UK service impact are confirmed. The ministerial numbers describe availability, not successful compromise; a public service may be offline because its operator is being cautious. The scale of breached UK systems remains unknown, and this is not evidence that all PHP sites are compromised.

Threat-to-household assessment: Indirect for most people. Server owners should inventory, update immediately, apply temporary mitigations or take exposed systems offline if patching is impossible, and monitor for compromise. Ordinary users should keep devices updated and be alert to phishing, but need no exceptional action.

Why it matters: This is targeted travel-health regulation, not evidence of a general vaccine emergency or UK outbreak. Travellers considering vaccination need an alternative product and continued mosquito precautions; people previously given IXCHIQ need no action if they have no side effects.

Evidence / uncertainty: Withdrawal and official instructions are confirmed. The MHRA has not established that every serious event was caused by the vaccine. Seek medical advice for suspected adverse effects and report through the Yellow Card scheme; do not stop or substitute any medicine based on headlines alone.

Threat-to-household assessment: Low for the general UK population; direct and time-sensitive for travellers arranging chikungunya protection. Follow a travel clinic or prescriber rather than attempting self-selection.
