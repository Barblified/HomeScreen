Current material risk cards

Budget energy relief — eligibility, timing and funding now confirmed

MODERATE cost pressure · easing signal · high confidence · months The final Budget provides a £2.6bn Household Energy Payment of £100 from January 2027 for up to 20 million English households in council-tax bands A–D, with Barnett funding for devolved governments. Electricity-VAT relief is extended to 1 July 2027 and is expected to lower the April 2027 annual price cap by about £50. The payment is funded by temporarily increasing the Bank Corporation Tax Surcharge from 3% to 6% for 15 months. Gas-standing-charge reform remains a separate Ofgem implementation task. [41][42][43][46][47]

Why it matters: The policy now has defined eligibility, timing and funding, reducing uncertainty for many households, although it is modest relative to wider energy and food-cost exposure.

Evidence / uncertainty: The Budget and HMRC collections are published. Ofgem methodology, operational payment guidance and equivalent devolved schemes remain implementation questions.

Household consequence: No immediate action. Eligible English households can note the January timetable; do not change tariffs or direct debits until operational guidance is issued.

Be Ready campaign — Commons scrutiny turns guidance into a delivery test

MODERATE preparedness gap · improving awareness · high confidence · weeks–years Security Minister Dan Jarvis told MPs that only 13% of people surveyed felt prepared for an emergency that could interrupt essential services. He said the three-year Be Ready campaign was not launched because of specific intelligence or an imminent threat. MPs challenged the Government on affordability, digital exclusion, vulnerable households, local-resilience coverage, telecoms backup and measurable take-up. [17][39][40]

Why it matters: Preparedness advice works only if people can afford, access and act on it, and if local services and communications remain available during disruption.

Evidence / uncertainty: Hansard is the direct parliamentary record and independent reporting confirms the practical checklist. The 13% figure is self-reported; campaign budget, success measures and local implementation detail remain unresolved.

Household consequence: Add essentials gradually, rotate normal food and water, keep paper contacts and check the Priority Services Register. Do not panic-buy or interpret the campaign as evidence of a specific imminent incident.
