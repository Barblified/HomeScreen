Infrastructure and utilities

MODERATE · STABLE Confidence: medium-high · Horizon: weeks–winter

What changed: No national outage was identified. Ofgem’s 16 October consultation on activating a market stabilisation charge is a signal of wholesale-market stress management, not a household supply failure. [8]

Why it matters: Power, water and communications are interdependent. A local outage can rapidly affect heating controls, card payments, mobile charging and water pumping even when national systems are sound.

Evidence / uncertainty: Government says winter maintenance and resilience measures are under way; performance under severe conditions remains untested. [13]

Practical implication: Test torches and alarms, charge power banks, and join Priority Services Registers if eligible. Never use outdoor combustion equipment indoors.

Food and essential supplies

MODERATE · COST PRESSURE RISING Confidence: medium · Horizon: weeks–months

What changed: No UK shortage is confirmed. The underlying cost channel remains adverse: disrupted Hormuz fertiliser flows, high diesel prices and attacks along several shipping routes. [11]

Why it matters: Fertiliser and fuel affect farming, processing and distribution with lags; price effects are more plausible than empty shelves in the near term.

Evidence / uncertainty: UN-linked warnings focus most acutely on vulnerable import-dependent countries. UK pass-through depends on duration, substitution and retailer contracts.

Practical implication: Maintain and rotate a modest household buffer. Buy normal foods you use; do not speculate in bulk stocks.

Cyber and communications

ELEVATED · STRATEGICALLY RISING Confidence: high · Horizon: ongoing

What changed: No specific nationwide communications outage was identified. The strategic assessment remains elevated after MI5 and NCSC warnings about hostile-state and disruptive activity against critical systems. [5] [6]

Why it matters: Household impacts are more likely to be phishing, account takeover, payment disruption or loss of access to cloud-only information than physical infrastructure destruction.

Evidence / uncertainty: Threat reporting identifies capability and intent, not a forecast that a particular household will be attacked.

Practical implication: Enable multi-factor authentication, install updates, keep offline recovery codes and contacts, and retain a battery radio and paper information layer.

What changed: The Flood and Drought Resilience Taskforce met on 14 October and government winter-readiness work continues. [12] [13]

Why it matters: Better cross-sector planning, maintenance and public guidance reduce the chance that a local incident cascades.

Evidence / uncertainty: Plans and investment are positive indicators; readiness must still be judged by execution during real stress. The 2026 National Risk Register remains the official all-hazards frame. [16]

Practical implication: Use official warning channels and local resilience information. Household preparation complements public systems; it does not replace them.

Practical household and EDC preparedness

MODERATE · STABLE Confidence: high · Horizon: now

What changed: No threat-driven equipment requirement emerged. The case remains for low-cost, multi-hazard basics rather than specialist gear.

Why it matters: The same simple items cover the most plausible short disruptions: power/water loss, severe weather, a journey delay, evacuation or communications failure.

Evidence / uncertainty: Prepare guidance recommends considering supplies for several days and 2.5–3 litres of drinking water per person per day as a survival minimum. [17]

Practical implication: Layer analogue/offline capability—paper contacts/maps, battery radio, replaceable-cell torch—alongside phones, GNSS and power banks.

Correlated risks

Gulf conflict → oil/diesel and freight → inflation and supply-chain pressure. This is the most material current cascade for ordinary UK households.
Hostile-state tension → cyber/hybrid activity → utilities, payments or communications disruption. The probability of a severe household event remains uncertain, but the cross-domain exposure justifies basic offline backups.
Winter weather → local power/transport interruption → health and communications strain. Current conditions are benign; resilience measures are worthwhile because consequences cluster when an outage occurs.

EDC / preparedness check

  • ALREADY COVERED: keep a charged phone, normal payment methods, house/vehicle keys and essential medication.
  • CHECK: torch operation, smoke/CO alarms, power-bank state, radio batteries and expiry/condition of first-aid contents.
  • CHECK: paper emergency contacts, local flood-warning registration where relevant, and eligibility for utility Priority Services Registers.
  • CONSIDER: 72 hours of rotating water and ordinary shelf-stable food, plus a small accessible cash reserve.
  • CONSIDER: vehicle visibility layer, warm clothing, water and a basic first-aid kit for longer autumn journeys.
