Current material risks

UPDATED

Energy affordability remains the dominant household exposure

The OBR says higher energy prices and interest rates raise borrowing and reduce near-term growth. Budget measures include extended electricity VAT relief and a future £100 payment for many English households.

Why it matters: Energy affects heating, transport, food distribution and discretionary income simultaneously.

Evidence / uncertainty: Primary documents define the measures; actual relief depends on eligibility and implementation. EU ministers report adequate supply but serious affordability pressure.

Household consequence: Plan around current bills, not announced future payments; compare tariffs carefully and use supplier hardship routes early.

Cascading interdependencies

Energy → inflation → rates → household cashflow: global fuel costs feed transport and heating; persistent inflation can delay rate relief and intensify debt stress. [1][2]

Shipping attacks → insurance / freight → goods and fuel: the current Black Sea incident is not a UK shortage, but repeated disruption can transmit through costs. [16][19]

Data breach → impersonation → financial loss: partial personal details can improve scam credibility even without a confirmed banking-data loss. [5][7]

EDC and preparedness checklist

  • Potable-water containers, labelled and rotated; do not reuse chemical containers.
  • One rechargeable head torch plus a replaceable-cell light and spare cells.
  • Phone power bank, cables and a mains charger; keep battery packs away from heat and damage.
  • Battery radio, paper contacts, essential documents and medication list.
  • First-aid and warmth layers; practise safe indoor fire and carbon-monoxide precautions.
  • Use tools and knives only for lawful utility purposes; public carry requires compliance with UK law.
