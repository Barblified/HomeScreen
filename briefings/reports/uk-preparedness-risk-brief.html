Current material risk cards

School safeguarding and social cohesion — government accepts antisemitism review

Low immediate · structural · weeks–months Sir David Bell's independent review found that almost three in five participating Jewish pupils aged 16–19 and half of Jewish teachers had experienced or witnessed antisemitism; about one in five pupils overall reported witnessing it. The government has accepted all 27 recommendations, including treating antisemitism as a distinct safeguarding risk, clearer political-impartiality guidance, teacher resources and an advice service for leaders. [42][43][44][45]

Household consequence: The immediate physical threat to an ordinary household is low, but affected pupils and staff may face real safeguarding harm. The implementation pathway runs through schools, colleges, inspectorates and reporting systems rather than household security measures.

Evidence / uncertainty: The report and government response are primary evidence; two independent outlets confirm the Commons response. Survey figures describe participating respondents rather than a census of every pupil. Funding, timetable, evaluation and the treatment of lawful classroom debate remain open.

Measured action: Use ordinary school safeguarding and complaints routes for specific incidents. Do not confront, profile or monitor people on the basis of identity or political viewpoint.

Energy and transport costs — oil price risk rises, physical flows still hold

Moderate · rising · weeks–months Brent rose 1.5% to $110.38 and US crude to $99.18 early Wednesday amid tanker attacks and a reported US inventory draw. ONS now confirms the transmission into September prices: motor fuels rose 29.8% year on year and made the largest contribution to CPI accelerating to 3.5%. Counter-signals are that Kpler data reported by the Wall Street Journal put Middle East oil flows above pre-war levels in the week to 18 October, and the UK has approved Jackdaw, with first gas possible from late October and peak output estimated at about 6% of current domestic production. [24][26][27][33][34][35]

Household consequence: The credible UK pathway is already price-led: vehicle fuel, freight and business inputs feeding transport and goods costs. Jackdaw may marginally strengthen supply but its scale is insufficient to guarantee a near-term bill reduction.

Escalate if: export volumes fall durably, terminals are damaged, or UK wholesale/forecourt prices accelerate further. De-escalate if: attacks subside, costs fall together and Jackdaw starts on schedule.

Measured action: Do not stockpile fuel. Consolidate discretionary trips where practical, maintain vehicles normally and watch verified UK price/supply notices.

Household cost and fiscal pressure — fresh inflation and borrowing deterioration

Moderate · rising · weeks–months September CPI rose to 3.5% from 3.1%, above the 3.4% Reuters-poll forecast; services inflation rose to 3.7% and core CPI to 2.8%. Separately, the public sector borrowed £24.5bn, £1.9bn above the OBR profile, while April–September borrowing was £97.8bn. Net debt exceeded £3tn in cash terms but fell year on year as a share of GDP to 94.0%. [27][28][29][30][31][32]

Household consequence: Motorists face the clearest direct increase. Broader effects could arrive through freight, interest rates and the 28 October Budget. Today’s figures do not determine tax, benefit or rate decisions; they narrow policymakers’ room.

Escalate if: core/services inflation rises again, pump prices climb, gilt yields remain high or the Budget withdraws essential support abruptly. De-escalate if: fuel prices reverse, underlying inflation softens and the OBR shows adequate fiscal headroom.

Measured action: Review routine fuel and debt-service exposure, preserve an emergency buffer where possible, and wait for confirmed Budget and Bank decisions before changing long-term plans.

IXCHIQ withdrawal — targeted travel-health action, not a general emergency

Low general · direct for affected travellers The MHRA has withdrawn IXCHIQ’s UK marketing authorisation after the Commission on Human Medicines concluded that its benefit-risk balance was no longer favourable. It must no longer be prescribed, supplied or administered. Serious reports included chikungunya-like illness and rare encephalitis, mainly in older or medically vulnerable people but also in some people without known conditions. [36][37]

Household consequence: The direct pathway is limited to people arranging chikungunya vaccination for travel or monitoring a suspected adverse effect. The MHRA says previous recipients need no action if they have no side effects; mosquito avoidance remains important and another licensed vaccine is available.

Escalate if: the regulator broadens the warning, publishes a new causal assessment or a clinician identifies a serious suspected reaction. De-escalate if: an affected traveller is switched safely to an alternative and no symptoms occur.

Measured action: Anyone booked for IXCHIQ should contact the travel clinic or prescriber. Seek medical advice for suspected side effects and use the Yellow Card scheme; do not self-substitute or infer a wider vaccine risk.

Sources: [36] [37]

Escalate if: verified local offence clusters, police warnings or repeated service gaps emerge. De-escalate if: published outcomes show disruption of offending and improved victim support without displacement.

Measured action: Record property identifiers, use lawful physical security and official Rural Watch/reporting channels; call 999 for immediate danger and never confront suspects.

  • Battery CO alarm where appropriate; tested smoke alarms.
  • Routine medicines and NHS details kept current.
  • Paper list of priority contacts and policy numbers.
