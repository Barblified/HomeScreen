Defence spending — Budget path promised, funding detail pending

Moderate long-term risk · rising fiscal commitment · medium confidence · months–years Burnham said Wednesday’s Budget will set out a route to 3% of GDP on core defence by 2030 and 3.5% by 2035, while securing £5 billion a year for the existing defence investment plan. NATO’s full commitment is 5%, with up to 1.5% covering resilience and security-related investment. Commons Library records an estimated 2.6% UK baseline for 2026; Reuters reports high borrowing costs and public debt constrain the Budget. [15][16][17][18]

Why it matters: Defence, infrastructure resilience and fiscal capacity compete for overlapping resources. A published path can improve planning, but only funded allocations and delivery milestones reduce capability risk.

Evidence / uncertainty: The target and baseline are official. The new commitment is an on-record statement reported from ITV; the Budget detail remains unknown until Wednesday.

Household consequence: No immediate action. Watch taxation, energy support and public-service trade-offs rather than treating the spending target as a direct domestic threat.

Policing accountability — Henry Nowak review begins

MODERATE structural risk · rising scrutiny · high confidence · months The Home Secretary commissioned Alex Chalk KC to lead an independent two-phase review into whether agencies missed opportunities before Henry Nowak’s murder and how the family was treated afterwards. Phase 1 covers cross-agency records, risk management, information sharing and criminal-justice support and must report within five months; Phase 2 follows the IOPC investigation and may examine the IOPC and police response. [8][9][30][31]

Why it matters: The review may expose or rule out systemic gaps spanning policing, health, local government, education and victim support—precisely the interfaces where weak information sharing can compound harm.

Evidence / uncertainty: Commission, scope and timetable are verified. It is not yet evidence that any agency failed; the review cannot determine criminal or civil liability and must avoid prejudicing the IOPC, inspection, inquest or other proceedings.

Household consequence: No immediate protective action. Treat inflammatory commentary as non-actionable; watch the Phase 1 report and any evidenced recommendations for local safeguarding or emergency-service practice.

Sources: [8] [9] [30] [31]
