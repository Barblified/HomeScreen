Environmental hazards / water

ELEVATED · DETERIORATING

Why it matters: Weak autumn/winter recharge could extend restrictions and agricultural pressure into 2027. Ardingly’s drought permit is a local warning, not evidence that national taps are about to fail.

Practical implication: Potable-water storage is the strongest evidence-linked household preparedness priority.

Economic / financial resilience

ELEVATED · MIXED / STABLE

What changed: Brent settled 0.06% lower at $102.25 and WTI 1.9% lower after European countries agreed to release diesel reserves; this reduces immediate refined-fuel pressure but does not remove it. The FTSE 100 recovered 0.32% on Friday yet lost 2.2% over the week, its steepest weekly fall since April, as the bond rout remained the main stress. [2][3]

Why it matters: UK 30-year gilt yields topped 6% on Thursday and higher bank funding costs are already feeding into mortgage pricing. The main chain remains fuel and energy → inflation → rates and financing → weaker household disposable income. Ofgem’s October–December cap is £1,723 for the illustrative typical direct-debit household, 4% above the previous quarter. [4][15]

Practical implication: Prioritise budget headroom and review upcoming fixed-rate expiries early; physical stockpiling does not solve this risk.
